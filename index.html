About

Computer vision researcher · Low-light image enhancement

I am an M.Tech scholar in Communication & Signal Processing at the National Institute of Technology Silchar. My research is on efficient deep learning for low-light image enhancement: recovering detail, colour and brightness from images taken in poor lighting.

I work across the full research loop: designing lightweight architectures, training them on GPUs, benchmarking them fairly against state-of-the-art methods, and analysing results carefully so that every claimed improvement holds up against random variation between training runs.

Before deep learning, my background was in electronics and digital hardware, including Verilog HDL, FPGA design and a VLSI internship.

NTR-Net: Neighbourhood Transform Refinement Network

A lightweight low-light image enhancement network. Instead of generating every output pixel, it predicts a grid of small local colour transforms and applies them to the dark input.

Contributions
  • Designed the network: 0.94 M parameters and 5.56 GMACs at 256×256, about 2.8× less compute than RetinexFormer.
  • Ran a headroom study before training: a perfect 16×16×8 transform grid reaches 30.3 dB on LOL-v1, confirming the idea has room to grow.
  • Error analysis with oracle corrections showed that most of the remaining error is per-image brightness and tone, not noise (+0.1 dB).
  • Ran multi-run ablation chains and one-factor-at-a-time probes, keeping only changes that beat run-to-run noise; a +0.77 dB result was traced to seed luck and dropped.
  • Tested generalisation on 109 unseen images from other cameras (LSRW and new LOL-v2 scenes).
  • Built automated training and evaluation pipelines on a local RTX 4050 and Kaggle GPUs.

M.Tech ResearchOngoing

NFR-v4 + HVI on RetinexFormer

Does a neighbourhood-attention refinement block (NFR) really improve a state-of-the-art enhancement model when both are trained the same way from scratch?

Audit of the earlier prototype
  • Resizing test images to 256×256 had inflated PSNR by 1.3 dB, and the block crashed on non-square images.
  • Rebuilt a full-resolution evaluation that reproduces the published RetinexFormer result (25.16 dB / 0.845).
Redesign
  • 4-head attention over the own patch plus 8 neighbours, relative-position bias, zero-initialised residual and an HVI-guided gate.
  • 59% fewer added parameters, verified with an automated CPU test suite before any GPU run.
Controlled A/B result · LOL-v2-Synthetic (100 images)
ModelPSNR (dB)SSIMGT-mean PSNR
RetinexFormer (baseline)25.210.93129.00
RetinexFormer + NFR-v425.950.93529.55

Both trained from scratch for 150K iterations with the official recipe and the same seed, in parallel on 2× T4 GPUs. On LOL-v1 the two models tie within seed variance.

Key finding
An error budget with paired standard errors shows that about 8 dB of the remaining error is a global tone curve, and all of NFR's gain lies there. HSV brightness augmentation kept 22.18 dB (vs 17.42 dB) when input exposure is halved.
